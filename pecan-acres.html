Pecan Acres — Lil' Coteau, Louisiana

"Pecan Acres ain't got a single pecan tree, bud. Got one pecan. Granddaddy keeps it in a jar."

The place

Pecan Acres is a trailer park at the end of Bayou Rouge Road, past the Exxon that sells boudin and lottery hopes, where the blacktop gives up and turns into shell gravel. Fourteen lots, eleven trailers, two boats on blocks that folks also live in, and one lot that's just a lawn chair everyone agrees belongs to "somebody." The ditch out front floods every April and the kids call it the Lagoon.

The whole park hums at night: bug zappers, a window unit on lot 6 that sounds like a man trying to start a mower inside a church, and frogs going full choir. Theo says growing up there is why he's tidesick for any place with a screen door.

Landmarks

  • Lot 9 — Granddaddy Royce's trailer. Seafoam green, a carport made of a billboard for a lawyer who's since been disbarred. Outboard motors hang off sawhorses like deer.
  • The Pecan Jar. One pecan, sealed in a pickle jar on Royce's porch since 1987. Nobody knows why. Touching it is bad luck.
  • Dale's Pre-Loved Marine. Cousin Dale's "lot" — a strip of mud by the canal with a hand-painted sign: MOSTLY FLOATS. FINANCING AVAILABLE (ASK DALE'S MAMA).
  • The Waffle Spot. Out on Hwy 90. Mama works the night grill. Open 24 hours, which in Lil' Coteau means the only light you can see from space.
  • Boudreaux Bait & Tackle. Where Theo got let go for naming the crickets. A cricket named Gary is still, legally, at large.
  • The Lagoon. The flooded ditch. Site of Theo's first boat (a cooler lid) and first heartbreak (Tammy Rae, watching from the bridge).
  • Deacon's Hole. A crawl space under lot 9 where Deacon the raccoon keeps shiny things and, once, a whole rotisserie chicken.

The locals

  • Granddaddy Royce — fixes outboard motors, believes every problem in life is a carburetor problem. Raised Theo.
  • Cousin Dale — used boat salesman, ponytail, permanent sunburn, unshakable optimism.
  • Mama — works nights at the Waffle Spot, tough as a cast-iron skillet, softer than she lets on.
  • Deacon — the family raccoon. Not a pet, "a member." Steals, judges, forgives.
  • Tammy Rae — Theo's middle-school crush. Moved to Shreveport. Theo still talks about Shreveport like it's the moon.
  • Miss Odile (lot 3) — sells pralines and prophecies, mostly wrong about both.
  • Mr. Fontenot (lot 12) — hasn't spoken a full sentence since 1994 but waves like he's landing planes.
  • The Thibodeaux twins — run the park's only business, renting out a trampoline by the hour.

Theo on home

Theo tells it like Pecan Acres is the center of the universe and also a place the universe forgot to check on. Every Shoal story he tells eventually swerves back to lot 9: "That's a flumbuckin' carburetor situation, is what that is."
